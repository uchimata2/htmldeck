# L-90 — A finding says where the weight is; it cannot say what removing it is worth

Learned 2026-08-14 (**T-145**, **T-146**, **T-147**). Three ranked findings from one audit were
implemented in one session, and **all three proposed a shape that the measurement changed** — while
all three were still worth doing. The pattern is not that the audit was careless. It is that a
finding is produced by *looking at sizes*, and a size tells you where the weight sits and nothing
about what it costs to move.

- One proposed extracting the **completed** rows of a table and leaving the open ones. The open ones
  were 57% of the section and the only part still growing, so that split would have rebuilt the
  finding by itself, on a timer.
- One led with **81×** — the whole document over one entry. That ratio is the cost of a whole-file
  read, and nothing in the project performed or instructed one. The work was still right, for a
  reason the finding never mentioned: the storage change made an unchecked citation interface
  checkable.
- One proposed **four files, one per phase**. Everything outside the target section was smaller than
  the target section, so four files would cost a reader more openings than the whole document did.

**How to apply.**

1. **Treat the proposed change as a hypothesis and the finding as an observation.** The observation —
   *this section is 61% of this file* — is usually sound and re-measurable. The remedy beside it was
   written without costing the alternatives.
2. **Re-measure before quoting the ratio, and ask what operation it describes.** A ratio of whole to
   part is only a saving if something reads the whole. Find the operation a session actually
   performs and price that.
3. **Ask which half has the growth rate**, not which half is bigger today. A cut that leaves the
   growing part behind buys one reduction and no rule.
4. **Let the real gain replace the stated one, in writing.** Twice here the work was worth doing for
   something the row did not claim. Say so in the closure and correct the band, or the next reader
   inherits a number the project has already disproved.
5. **A finding whose remedy does not survive is not a finding refuted.** Separating the two is what
   lets you keep the observation and drop the plan without arguing about either.
