# L-92 — A share-of-file figure is a claim about a unit, and the instrument has to count that unit

Learned 2026-08-14 (**T-150**). A context audit reported three files as docstring outliers —
`build_probes.py` at **85%**, `print_variants.py` at **58%**, `audit.py` at **36%**, against a corpus
figure of **30%** — and ranked a finding on them. Re-measured by AST role, the same files are
**3.2%**, **15.3%** and **11.6%**, against **16.3%**. The instrument had counted every triple-quoted
**string token** and called the result docstrings.

**The three files it named are the three where that is most wrong.** They hold their payloads as
triple-quoted constants: `PROBE_JS`, `PROBE_HTML`, `PROBE_3D_HTML`, `PRINT_PROBE_HTML` in the first,
`PAGINATED` and `REFLOW` in the second. Those strings are not prose about the tool, they are what the
tool writes out. The "85%" file is the **least** docstring-heavy of 38, and the one action its own
numbers describe — move that text to `docs/research/` — would have deleted two tools' output.

**Why it survived being written down.** The figures were precise, consistent with each other, and
reproducible: run the same script and get 85% again, forever. They matched the corpus figure the
audit stated elsewhere, so the outliers looked like a tail of a distribution the reader had already
accepted. **Nothing about a wrong unit looks like an error from inside the number.** Four figures
agreed, and all four were about something else.

**The tell that was available.** *Docstrings are 30% of a Python tree* is a surprising claim about a
codebase, and *one file is 85% docstring* is an extraordinary one. A figure that would be remarkable
if true is the figure to re-derive, not the one to lead with.

**How to apply.**

1. **Name the unit in the sentence that reports the share** — "docstrings by AST role", not
   "docstrings". A share with an unnamed unit cannot be checked by a reader and will be quoted
   onward by everyone who reads it. This one reached five documents.
2. **Bind on the structure that defines the unit, not on the syntax that usually accompanies it.**
   Quote style is a proxy for *kind* and holds right up until a file uses that syntax for something
   else; `ast.get_docstring` is the kind itself. The choice of instrument is a claim about the
   artefact, and it is the claim nobody restates when quoting the number (**L-36**).
3. **Check the instrument against a case whose answer you know by construction** before reading its
   output as a finding (**L-86**). Ten bytes of docstring and twenty of payload is enough: it
   reproduced the 3.5× overstatement in a nine-line file.
4. **Take the second reading with a differently-shaped instrument** (**L-62**). Counting the same
   thing twice by the same method measures its blind spot twice. What identified the conflation here
   was measuring *both* quantities at once and finding the audit's numbers in the other column.
5. **Re-measure the cheap rows too.** This was rank 13 of 13, the row nobody would spend an hour on
   — which is exactly why the error would have outlived every finding above it.

Related: **L-90** — a finding can say where the weight is and not what removing it is worth; this is
one step further back, where it could not say where the weight was. **L-86** on checking a scan
against a known answer, **L-62** on the second reading needing a different instrument, **L-36** on a
measurement choice being a claim about the instrument, and **L-82** on a band being a claim about
reachable saving rather than about the size of a surface.
