# L-139 — A measurement taken on one subject and written down as general is a claim nothing will re-check; name the subject in the sentence or the conclusion outlives it

A comparison run on one deck, one document, one deck's worth of markup answers a question about
*that* subject. Written into a comment as a property of the tool, it becomes a fact the next reader
inherits and has no reason to distrust — and the sentence carries no trace of how narrow its
evidence was.

Measured 2026-08-29 closing
[T-261](../../tasks/T-261-ds-035-measures-a-text-run-through-its-transform.md). `audit.PROBE` was
left unpinned by [T-209](../../tasks/T-209-six-more-probes-measure-a-page-whose-entrance-never-ran.md)
with a comment reading *its geometry rows were measured both ways on the portfolio deck and are
identical, so nothing is bought by pinning this one*. Re-run with the same method:

| Subject | Keys differing, pinned against unpinned |
| :--- | :--- |
| the portfolio deck — T-209's own | **0 of 65** |
| a deck whose `scaleY(0)` entrance has not started | **3 of 66** |

T-209's half is exactly right and reproduces on demand. The other half — *nothing is bought* — was
never measured, because there was nothing to measure it against. Two geometry rows moved on the
second deck, one of them reporting three display-size headings at `0 du` under a rule that says
*text below 16 design units*, which is what an adopter spent a session chasing.

**The tell is a sentence with a subject in its evidence and none in its conclusion.** *Measured both
ways on the portfolio deck* and *nothing is bought by pinning this one* sit in one sentence, and only
the first half can be falsified.

**How to apply.**

1. **Put the subject in the conclusion, not only in the evidence.** *Identical on the portfolio deck*
   is durable and invites the second measurement. *Nothing is bought* forecloses it.
2. **A property claimed of a tool needs a subject that could have disagreed.** One deck whose
   entrances happen not to move the axis the rule reads is not a sample; it is the absence of one.
   Choose the second subject for how it differs, not for being handy.
3. **Re-derive before building on it.** T-261's own scope named the re-derivation as worth more than
   the fix, and it was: the fix is a reordering, the correction is a defect class. When a task's
   inputs include *this contradicts something recorded as settled*, measure the settled thing first.
4. **This is [L-138](L-138.md)'s failure along the other axis.** There the sample was too small in
   *runs* against a rate; here it is too narrow in *subjects* against a population. Both produce a
   green that means nothing, and both read as measured work.
5. **Search for the shape rather than the instance.** Any *measured both ways and identical* — or
   *checked and it makes no difference* — written without naming what it was checked on is the same
   claim, whatever it is about.
