# L-98 — A tool that publishes its own measurements is still publishing its own measurements

Caught 2026-08-14 (**T-136**), screening a named tool into a technique catalogue. The tool ships a
decision ladder the agent applies before writing code, and it advertises **−54% code, −22% tokens,
−20% cost, −27% time**. An independent paired benchmark of **80 tasks** measured **−15% code**,
**−10.3% cost** and **−11% time** — and only the cost result was statistically significant. **The
advertised effect is about four times the measured one.**

**The number that mattered was not the size of the gap but its shape.** The benchmark also found the
saving reaches −31% on big builds and is *zero* where the agent was going to write almost nothing. A
single headline figure hid a technique that pays on one kind of task and does nothing on another —
which is exactly the question a screening verdict turns on. Screening on the advertised figure would
have adopted it for a project whose write volume is documents.

**A self-published measurement is evidence about the publisher's best case**, and that is not
dishonesty — it is the case they built it for. Treat it as a claim with a known bias, not as a
measurement.

**How to apply.**

1. **Look for a second measurement before screening a named tool**, and prefer one whose author did
   not ship the tool. A benchmark with a sample size and a p-value outranks a landing page.
2. **If no independent measurement exists, say so in the row** and screen on the mechanism instead of
   the number. *Unmeasured* is a verdict; borrowing the vendor's figure is not.
3. **Ask where the effect concentrates**, not just how big it is. A mean over a benchmark can be the
   average of a large effect and no effect, and which half your work resembles decides the verdict.
4. **The same discipline applies to this repository's own figures.** Every measurement here was taken
   by the party proposing the change — which is why they carry the command that reproduces them
   (**L-95**) rather than only the result.
