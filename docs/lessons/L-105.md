# L-105 — A feature that widens what an artefact holds re-points every rule that reads it whole

Met 2026-08-16 (**T-167**). T-070 let a deck carry its sources inside itself, as
`template.qv-src`. One rule was amended for it — DS-110, so a quoted screenshot stops being a
rasterised diagram. **Thirty-five others were not, and every one of them is a lambda over the whole
file.** So the day a deck quoted a document somebody else wrote, DS-100 failed it for a question in
the source's section heading, and the figure binder attributed **122 of 152 figures** to a deck that
had written none of them.

**Nothing was broken by the feature, and nothing was broken by the rules.** Each rule kept doing
exactly what it always did; what changed underneath them is what *the file* means. Before T-070 the
deck's bytes were the deck's claims, so *the whole file* and *what the deck says* were the same set,
and no rule had to choose between them. The feature split those two apart and left every rule
pointing at the older one.

**The two shipped decks could never have shown this.** They carry quick views and they pass, because
sources written inside this repository happen to contain no question headings and no figure that
disagrees with the slide quoting it. The gate was green for a year on a distinction it could not
draw — **L-36** with a delayed fuse: not a check nobody saw fail, but a check nobody could see fail
until the content arrived from outside.

**So the cost of such a feature is a sweep, and the sweep is the work.** Fixing T-167 was two rows
and one line. Deciding *which* two of thirty-six was the whole task, and the answer had no family
shape to it — the rules that keep the whole file are the ones about construction, because a quoted
source that reaches the network is a real defect wherever it sits, and the rules that take the cut
are the ones about copy. Three rows that read like candidates were left alone with reasons written
down, because a rule moved on a hunch is the same defect facing the other way.

**The tell is an amendment that names one rule.** When a change to what an artefact may contain
arrives with a single rule updated, that is not the scope of the change — it is the first rule
somebody happened to trip over. Ask what else reads the artefact whole, and write the classification
down, because the next person cannot tell a deliberate exception from an oversight.
