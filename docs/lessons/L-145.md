# L-145 — A rule's clean record is only as wide as the selector its instrument used, and the two are written in different files

[L-36](L-36.md) says the denominator travels in the line, so *0 off, of 0* and *0 off, of 8* cannot
read alike. [L-144](L-144.md) says a rule that has never fired cannot be protected by its prior
count. **This is the third case, and the most comfortable one to miss: the rule has fired, it has a
prior count, and the count is green — because the instrument was looking at part of the population
and the verdict line had no way to say so.**

Found twice in one batch, 2026-08-29, in
[T-245](../../tasks/T-245-seven-tool-defects-in-tools-deck.md) and
[T-269](../../tasks/T-269-three-build-path-defects-the-adopter-worked-around.md).

**First instance — a selector narrower than the rule.** `figgrid.py`'s probe took
`querySelector('.body svg.fig')`, the **first** diagram on each slide, and reported *n of m* where
`m` was what it had looked at. DS-236 read *0 of 9* on a deck carrying **ten** diagrams. Fixing the
selector to iterate did not just find one more figure: the tenth was the right-hand figure of a
two-column slide, correctly placed, and it failed the rule by **+962.3 du** — so the rule as written
could not be satisfied by a correct figure, and nobody had found out because nothing had ever
measured one. The rule's own history said *18 of 21 re-cut and gated since 2026-08-19*; that 21 was
what the blind probe could see, not what the decks held.

**Second instance — a count narrower than the number beside it.** DS-075's probe read
`scrollWidth` off `#doc` and counted overflowing elements over `#docBody *`. An element between the
two — a provenance row — held the page open while the verdict line read `overflowing: 0` and the
*widest* list printed nothing. The failure named a number with nothing beside it, and the count did
not merely omit the culprit: **it contradicted the measurement it was standing next to.**

**Why this survives review.** The rule lives in a design document and the population lives in a
selector in a probe, often a different repository directory and always a different language. A
reader checking the rule reads prose that is true. A reader checking the probe reads a selector that
looks like a reasonable way to find a diagram. Neither reading asks *is this every one*, because the
verdict line reports a denominator and a denominator feels like an answer to exactly that question.

**How to apply.**

1. **Read the rule's population out of the instrument, not out of the rule.** The prose says what
   ought to be measured; the selector says what is. When they differ, the selector is the rule.
2. **`querySelector` in a gate is a defect until argued otherwise.** Singular where the rule is
   plural is the whole of both instances above. If one is genuinely intended, say why in the line
   above it.
3. **A count and the measurement it accompanies must be taken over the same subtree.** A count over
   a smaller one can contradict its own number, which is worse than no count: it tells the reader
   the rule looked and found nothing.
4. **Report what was declined, by name and reason.** After the fix, `figgrid.py` prints
   *(declined 6: 6 icon)*. Six figures had been invisible in both numbers on a deck whose row read
   as complete. A skip nobody prints is a skip nobody can audit.
5. **When a widened instrument first fails, suspect the rule before the subject.** The new failure
   here was a correctly placed figure. The instinct is to fix what the gate now points at; the
   question is whether the rule ever covered it. Here it had not, and the answer was a ruling —
   a diagram answers to the text column it sits in — not a re-cut deck.
