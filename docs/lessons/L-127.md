# L-127 — A figure can be arithmetically right and relationally wrong, and only the drawing shows which

A generated chart has two kinds of truth in it, and they are checked in completely different places.

- **Arithmetic** — the parts sum to the total, the movements close on the closing balance, the five
  shares make 100. This is a property of the **model**, and a self-test over the model settles it.
- **Relation** — this connector leaves the bar it belongs to, this label names the nearest node,
  this axis stops at the node it runs into. This is a property of the **drawing**, and no amount of
  model testing reaches it.

Measured 2026-08-21. A twelve-slide deck passed 22 arithmetic identities, three geometry gates and a
121-rule checker, and carried **fifteen** relational defects across six of its figures. The waterfall
is the clean case: its self-test proved the movements close on 2,400, and they did — while every
connector between the bars was drawn at the *next* bar's top edge rather than at the running total
after the current one. The number was right. The picture was wrong. Nothing in the model could tell.

**Why the arithmetic passing is what makes it dangerous.** A figure that fails its own sums looks
broken and gets fixed. A figure whose sums are right reads as verified, so the drawing is never
questioned — and a reviewer who has seen the self-test go green looks at the picture less carefully,
not more.

**How to apply.**

1. **Name which kind of truth a check covers**, in the check. A suite called "the identities this
   deck promises" that holds only model arithmetic is claiming more than it tests.
2. **Check a relation on the emitted output, never by recomputing it.** A check that re-derives the
   placement from the same inputs shares the placement's bug and passes. Read the drawn marks back —
   the rectangles, the lines, the text anchors — and assert the relation between them.
3. **State the relation in the units the reader sees.** "The connector's `y` equals the running
   total after its bar" is a model statement and can be satisfied by a wrong drawing. "The
   connector sits on the edge that both its bars share" is a statement about the picture, and it
   fails on exactly the defect.
4. **Seed the defect back and watch the check fire.** A relational check written after the fix has
   never seen the thing it was written for. Restoring the old placement took four lines and proved
   both new checks caught what they claimed — one of them naming the two labels the reviewer had
   named, which is stronger evidence than either alone.
5. **Expect the drawing's defects to arrive in bulk.** Relations are not independent: the same
   figure vocabulary places labels, connectors and axes, so one blind spot yields many. Fifteen
   defects, no instrument having found one, is the shape to expect rather than the exception.

The general shape: **a model test and a drawing test are not two strengths of the same check, they
are checks of different things** — and a deck ships the drawing. Related: **L-01**, since looking is
what found all fifteen, and **L-13**, since the temptation to recompute rather than read back is
exactly the second copy of one quantity.
