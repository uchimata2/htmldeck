# L-163 — A ruling that changes the grain of a record re-aims the schedule and orphans every estimate written at the old grain

Found 2026-09-02 in cycle 42 of
[T-219](../../tasks/T-219-pre-release-audit-of-the-whole-repository.md), the pre-release audit's
grading pass, which measures what the run predicted against what it cost.

The audit raised 129 findings and gave each one a **rank** and an **effort**. Cycle 40's triage then
ruled — correctly, and for a good reason — that a severity obliges *a disposition before the
release* rather than one record per finding. So the eight `High` rows became eight tasks, the `Med`
rows were grouped by shared subject and remedy, and the whole `Low` band became a single task of 49.
**The grain of the record changed from the finding to the task**, and two things downstream inherited
that change without anyone deciding they should.

- **The effort column lost its subject.** Effort was predicted 129 times and is testable **13** times
  — only where a task closed exactly one finding. The other 116 have a cost recorded per task, so the
  per-finding figure has nothing to face and never will. **90% of a column was made unfalsifiable by
  a ruling about something else**, and the ruling is still right.
- **The rank started scheduling the work.**
  [`REMEDIATION-ORDER.md`](../REMEDIATION-ORDER.md) §1 states plainly that severity does *not*
  sequence the remediation — that order measures rework. The closing dates say otherwise: every
  `High` finding closed in the first two days, 50 of 51 `Low` findings on the last, and it survives
  holding the aspect fixed. The order never ranked a batch by severity. It did not have to: a band
  that is one task is one slot, so the granularity ruling chose the schedule **one level below where
  the order document was looking**.

**Neither effect is visible from the ruling.** Cycle 40 decided how many records to write. It did not
mention estimates, and it did not mention order, because at that altitude it had nothing to do with
either. Both consequences are only visible from a reader that joins the register to the tasks — which
is the grading pass, run at the end, when nothing can be changed.

**How to apply.** When a ruling changes the unit a record is kept in, **name what already reads that
record and say what happens to each**. Two questions cover most of it:

- *What was estimated at the old grain?* An estimate survives only where a record at the same grain
  will answer it. **Cost the unit that will be worked, not the unit that is found** — or accept, in
  writing, that the column is a note rather than a prediction.
- *What schedules by this unit?* A grouping key becomes an ordering key the moment the group is the
  thing a queue holds. A document that says *this attribute does not decide the order* is describing
  its own intent, not the behaviour, and it cannot see a grouping decision taken elsewhere.

The related failure is [L-136](L-136.md), where a count stood in for a membership; here the
membership was fine and the **unit** moved under it. [L-95](L-95.md)'s move applies to the salvage:
where a per-item estimate can no longer be graded, date the column and say what it was for, rather
than leaving 116 unfalsifiable predictions reading as measurements.
