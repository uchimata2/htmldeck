# L-157 — Where one step names two homes and only one of them has a proof, the unprovable half stops running and the proof stays green

Found 2026-09-02 in [T-237](../../tasks/T-237-the-release-machinery-and-its-record.md), closing
`PR-24`. [`PUBLISHING.md`](../PUBLISHING.md) §8 step 8 asked a release to record the shipping version
in **both** chronology homes, and stated one proof: *the version appears in the record without
anyone reconstructing it later*. [`RELEASE-HISTORY.md`](../RELEASE-HISTORY.md) satisfies that alone,
and `chronology.py` fails the run without its row. The other named home had nothing holding it, so
recording into it quietly stopped — for **85** tasks, while every release passed step 8.

- **A conjunction inherits the weakest proof, not the strongest.** *Do A and B, and here is how you
  know* reads as one step with one check. If the check only sees A, the step is A with B written
  beside it, and it degrades to exactly that the first time anyone is in a hurry.
- **The green is the damage.** Nobody ignored the step: it passed, every time, honestly, on the half
  it could measure. There was no moment at which somebody chose to skip B, so there was no moment at
  which anybody could have noticed.
- **The two homes were not the same kind of thing, and the proof is what exposed it.** One is a
  chronology, one is a decision record; a release writes a row into the first and nothing into the
  second. The half that could not be proved could not be proved *because it was not a step* — which
  is the useful signal. An unprovable half of a step is first a question about whether the step is
  one step.
- **The fix is to drop the half or to give it its own proof — never to restate the obligation.**
  Back-filling the 85 rows was the register's own suggested remedy and would have been false again
  inside a week, because nothing new would be watching. [L-135](L-135.md) is the same shape one layer
  up: derive the membership or expect the hand-kept list to decay.
