# L-147 — Duplicated data does not only cost bytes; it answers for the thing it was copied from, and the checkers reading it go green

Found 2026-08-30 in [T-233](../../tasks/T-233-a-shipped-deck-carries-eleven-copies-of-one-source.md),
closing `PR-83`. `portfolio-review.html` carried **eleven** byte-identical copies of one quick-view
payload, ten of them unreachable — 84,750 bytes, 20.8% of the deck. The finding was written as
waste. **Removing the waste turned three checkers red, and every one of them had been passing on
the dead copies.**

- `quickview.py`'s `rewire()` substituted with `count=1`, so a repair went into the **first**
  template while `deck.js` keys `qvSrc` by `data-qv` and renders the **last**. The verb reported a
  successful write over a deck that still showed the stale copy.
- The same tool's `check` compared the first template too, so a drift seeded into the copy the deck
  actually renders reported `match` and exited 0 — the detection
  [T-181](../../tasks/T-181-nothing-detects-that-a-decks-embedded-quick-view-has-drifted-from-its-source.md)
  shipped, defeated a second way.
- `spec.py`'s `SPEC-5` asks whether a slide **shows** a ledger figure, and a quick view's
  `<template>` sits inside its slide's own `<section>`. The payload is the *source document*, so a
  figure the source states and the slide never prints read as shown. **On two ledger rows.**

**The mechanism is the same each time and it is not about quick views.** A copy of a document placed
inside the thing that cites it makes every text search over that thing ambiguous, and a checker that
searches text cannot tell which copy answered. The copies were not merely inert — they were
*standing in* for the original.

**So deleting redundancy is a detection method, and its new reds are findings.** Isolate the change
to one variable before reading them: with the deck at `HEAD` and nothing altered but the
deduplication, `SPEC-5` failed on exactly the two rows the batch's gate had named — which is what
turned *my edit broke something* into *this was never being checked*. What that then exposed was a
real defect in a shipped deck, `65%` appearing nowhere a reader can see while the specification, the
foundation and the source all call it half the deck's argument
([T-282](../../tasks/T-282-the-opening-slide-carries-one-of-the-two-figures-its-specification-names.md)).

**And the fix for a checker reading inert markup must match what the writer emits, not the shape of
the tag.** The first form was `<template\b.*?</template>`, which is obviously right and is not: that
deck holds **eight** `<template` openings against **two** closings, six of them the quick-view
contract quoted in the shell's own prose. The pattern ran from one of those to the first real
closing tag, swallowed slide 1's `<section>` opening, and `SPEC-5` reported *slide 1 does not exist*
about a deck that has one — a rule made **blinder** by the fix meant to sharpen it, and blind in the
direction that fails loudly only by luck. It now matches `quickview.py`'s own `carried()` pattern,
and `spec.self_test` seeds an unclosed prose mention ahead of a slide.
