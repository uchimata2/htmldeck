# L-93 — A rule in five documents is a rule with no home

Found 2026-08-14 by [T-144](../../tasks/T-144-give-each-cumulative-rule-one-operative-home.md), which
was raised to delete copies and found there was nothing to delete *to*.

*A phase is not a version* was stated operatively in five documents — the always-loaded instructions,
the task workflow, the board preamble, the phase decision record and a document created that same
morning. **The release sequence, whose own step the original failure broke, said *bump the version*
and never said to what.** The rule was in every document that might need it and absent from the one
that governs the act.

**Restatement is the symptom; the missing home is the disease.** A rule nobody can point at gets
copied by whoever needs it next, and each copy is written by someone acting correctly. Counting the
copies measures how many people needed it, not how badly anyone behaved — which is why deleting them
without declaring a home regenerates them. The sixth copy here was written by the **sibling task**,
hours before the survey that found it, in a pass raised to remove duplicates.

**A rule that resists a single home is usually two rules.** These were: *which number a release
takes*, which binds when somebody tags, and *which field a task carries*, which binds when somebody
edits front-matter. Held together they had no possible owner, because no document governs both
moments. Split, each had an obvious one, and one of the two homes had to be written from scratch.

**How to apply.**

1. **Ask which document governs the act the rule constrains** — the moment somebody does the thing —
   before counting copies or deleting any.
2. **If no document does, that is the finding.** Write the rule there first. Deleting the copies is
   the cheap half and it comes second.
3. **If no single document can govern it, count the rules.** Two moments means two rules; give each
   its own home and let the others point.
4. **Tier 1 keeps the distinction and loses the story.** A pointer is weaker than a statement, so
   what stays where it is paid for on every turn is the sentence a reader must not act without —
   never the incident, which belongs in one lesson entry. [`L-13`](L-13.md) is the general form of
   this; **L-69** is the incident these five copies were all retelling.
