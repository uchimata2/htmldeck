# L-136 — A count with no membership cannot be audited, and its errors cancel instead of showing

A table that reports **how many** items are in each group, without ever saying **which**, looks like
a measurement and behaves like a claim. The counts can be added, compared and cited; none of that
touches whether they are true, because the set they describe was never written down.

**Two properties make this worse than an ordinary stale figure.**

- **A wrong count cannot be located, only re-derived.** A dead link names the thing it cannot find,
  so the search starts where the defect is. A count that is one too low names nothing at all. The
  only way to find out where is to reconstruct the membership from scratch — at which point the
  table was never the source, and never could have been.
- **Errors of opposite sign hide each other.** Two groups counted twice and three items counted by
  nobody net out to a discrepancy of one. The aggregate is the *residue* of the mistakes, never
  their size, and it always understates. A small discrepancy is therefore not evidence of a small
  problem — it is no evidence at all.

**Measured 2026-08-23.** [T-219](../../tasks/T-219-pre-release-audit-of-the-whole-repository.md)
planned a forty-three-cycle audit whose section 1 gave the tree as 492 files and whose section 2's
thirty-seven sized cycles summed to 491 — a gap of one file, raised as `PR-06`. Deriving the
membership from a rule per cycle
([T-223](../../tasks/T-223-derive-the-audit-cycles-membership-instead-of-counting-it.md)) showed the
plan covered at most **488 distinct** files: `shell/README.md` was counted in two cycles, and one
cycle claimed two files its own subject line cannot name. **Four files were unread, not one.** The
finding, stated honestly from the aggregate, understated itself four to one.

**The half that could be located was located to the byte** — cycle 16 stated 278,973 bytes and
derived 272,593, and the difference is 6,380, which is `shell/README.md` exactly. The half that could
not stays unlocatable: no pair of tracked files matches the 14,421 bytes cycle 4 claims beyond the
four its subject names, at the plan's own commit or today. One defect of each kind, in one table.

**How to apply.**

1. **State the rule that selects the members, and print the count from it.** The count then cannot
   disagree with the membership, because it is not a second fact.
2. **Give the reader a command that lists the members**, not only the total. *Which files does cycle
   7 read* has to be answerable in one run, or nobody will check the number against anything.
3. **Force a verdict on every item.** Assigned to exactly one group, or reported and failing — the
   partition `tools/check_all.py` keeps over checkers. Silence about an item is how it goes missing.
4. **Do not trust a small discrepancy.** Before reconciling two totals that nearly agree, derive one
   of them properly. Nearly-agreeing totals are the expected output of several errors, not the
   signature of one.
5. **Derived membership does not mean derived boundaries.** Where a boundary is a judgement — a size
   band, an id range — declare it and derive only what falls under it. A boundary that recomputes
   itself changes what a finished group contained, and any record of what was done to that group
   stops being true.
