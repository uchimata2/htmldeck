Move the window, not the fleet

One depot, one sorter line, and a nightly window that closes at 01:00.

05 · The cause

Sorting ends an hour late

One night at the depot, from the first trunk to the last round out.

InputThe second trunk carries 7,200 parcels and the line is rated at 3,100 an hour.
Step7,200 ÷ 3,100 is 2h 19m of sorting, at a rate held within 4% across a shift.
Result23:40 plus 2h 19m is 01:59, which is 59 minutes past the cut-off.
ExcludedReturns and same-day services, which do not pass this line.

7,200 parcels land at 23:40 and clear the sorter at 01:59, an hour past the 01:00 cut-off.

06 · The cause

Loading order, not distance

Four districts, one shared scale, ordered as the vans are loaded.

Ashgrove6 rounds, first away at 04:10. Loaded first on every night sampled.
Cranleigh5 rounds, first away at 04:35. The longest drive and the second-lowest miss rate.
Dellow4 rounds, first away at 05:05. Nearest to the depot and third from last.
Beacon Hill7 rounds, first away at 05:30. Loaded last on 27 of 31 peak nights.

The last district loaded misses 18.7%, and the district furthest away is loaded second and misses 11.2%.

10 · The cost

Six people work at 02:30

Two things this recommendation costs, said before anyone asks.

A shift pattern, not a line item

A six-person crew works 22:00 to 02:30, five nights a week through peak. The $170k is what that costs; the disruption is not in the number.

The saving rests on someone else

The carrier has never had to meet a 21:50 slot. The 02:30 cut-off is the hedge against the nights it does not, and it is why the crew exists.

The slotMissed on more than 5% of nights, and the second cut-off absorbs what the crew can load.
VolumeAbove 31,900 in a day, and the first trunk's sort runs into the second's.
The lineBelow 3,100 an hour, and every finish time in this deck moves later.

The window costs less because six people work at 02:30, and it needs the carrier to meet 95% of slots.
