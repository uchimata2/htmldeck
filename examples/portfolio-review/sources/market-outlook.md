# Core-market power price outlook, 2026–2030

Illustrative. The market, the curve and the supply figures below are constructed for a worked
example and describe no real market. Every value is an output of the assumptions stated with it.

## The forward curve

Modelled wholesale power price in the fund's core market, calendar year average:

| Year | $/MWh |
| :--- | ---: |
| 2026 | **78** |
| 2027 | 74 |
| 2028 | 69 |
| 2029 | 64 |
| 2030 | **61** |

The curve falls **22%** from 2026 to 2030. That is the external change, and it is the reason the
timing of this review is not arbitrary.

## Why it falls

**14.2 GW** of new generation is contracted to reach commercial operation in the market between
2027 and 2029, against modelled demand growth of **3.1 GW** over the same window. The gap is
supply the market has already committed to and cannot withdraw.

## What the fund's renewables assets earn

**61%** of the fund's renewable generation is sold under contract at a fixed price; the remaining
**39%** is exposed to the curve above. Weighted across the portfolio, a 22% fall in the wholesale
price moves renewables revenue by **−8.6%** by 2030.

The contracted share is not stable either: contracts covering **$310M** of NAV reach their
re-contracting date before 2030, and they were struck when the curve was above $80/MWh.

## The timing point, stated plainly

The fund's largest sector is at its highest ever share of NAV, and its uncontracted revenue basis
is modelled to fall by roughly a fifth over four years. Those two facts arrive together, and that
is what makes this a decision for this meeting rather than the next one.
