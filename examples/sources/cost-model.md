# Riverbend capital decision — cost model

Illustrative. Every figure below is an output of the assumptions stated with it.

## The grant

The state corridor grant is **$5.6M** capital, available once, and it lapses if it is not committed
in the award window. The award runs on a three-year cycle, so the next one lands in 2030 [est.].

## Proposal A — bike-share, 62 stations

| Line | Value | Basis |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| Capital | **$5.6M** | the whole grant; 62 stations at the vendor's per-station price |
| Operating, net | **$1.9M** a year | gross operating less user revenue |
| User revenue nets out | **$0.6M** | fares and memberships, at the modelled ridership |
| Grant left over | none | the whole grant is spent on capital |

Bike-share nets out user revenue at **$0.6M**, which is why its operating line is the smaller of
the two. That is the comparison's strongest point for the proposal and it is stated first.

## Proposal B — bus frequency, 6 trunk routes

| Line | Value | Basis |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| Capital | **$4.1M** | nine buses, depot works and signal priority |
| Grant left over | **$1.5M** | held for bike-share in the corridors where it wins |
| Operating | **$6.8M** a year | driver hours at the frequency the schedule needs |

Frequency capital of **$4.1M** leaves **$1.5M** of the grant uncommitted. The operating line is the
uncomfortable one: the general fund carries **$6.8M** a year, and no grant covers operating.

## The operating ceiling

The finance office's ceiling on new operating commitments is **$7.5M** a year. Operating cost
passes **$7.5M** if driver hours run above the modelled schedule, and that is the third tripwire on
the review gate. The modelled operating cost is **$6.8M** a year against that ceiling.

## What the city forgoes

Taking the frequency package means the city takes **$1.5M** less from the state than it could have
taken, because the bike-share proposal would have absorbed the whole **$5.6M**. Stated plainly
rather than left for someone to work out.
